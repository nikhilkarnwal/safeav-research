Foundations

Predicting a world.
Choosing an action.

A world model predicts scene evolution or a future representation. A vision-language-action model maps visual and language context to actions or trajectories. Either can exist without the other.

Illustrative integrated architecture
Observations + route / instruction
Shared scene representation
Candidate actions→Predicted consequences
Trajectory selection → control → next observation
A conceptual integration pattern; individual papers use different training and inference pathways.

Representation is a design choice

Predictions may be pixels, BEV/occupancy, actor trajectories, or compact latent features. Visual fidelity, planning utility, and counterfactual reliability are separate properties.

Language alignment is also a design choice

Understanding an instruction does not establish that the executed action follows it. SimLingo explicitly studies language-action alignment. Author source

Architecture landscape

Four ways prediction meets control.

01

Prediction for planning

Forecast scenes or actor states to inform trajectory selection. Test prediction accuracy and downstream decisions separately.

02

Imagined policy training

Learn dynamics, then train policies from latent rollouts. Think2Drive and Raw2Drive illustrate this family.

03

VLA action generation

Align visual and language representations with driving outputs. SimLingo provides a reactive simulator reference.

04

Future-informed VLA

Use future-image supervision, shared latent imagination, or feature alignment. DriveVLA-W0, DriveWorld-VLA, and RAF-VLA take different routes.

Selected recent methods

What each approach adds.

Foundational methods are included alongside recent work to explain the progression. Publication labels are limited to verified venue records; other entries are identified as preprints.

2024 · ECCV

Think2Drive

World-model RL

Reported contribution. Learns environment transitions and uses latent rollouts for efficient planner training.

Our assessment. A strong model-based RL reference, but privileged inputs make sensor-policy comparisons unequal.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Compact latent dynamics; privileged state. Authors report three days of training on one A6000; not an inference latency figure.

Paper; reproduction scope must be checked.

2025 · CVPR

SimLingo

VLA policy

Reported contribution. Combines vision-only driving, language understanding, and language-action alignment.

Our assessment. A relevant frozen-policy baseline. Action Dreaming should not be equated with a learned reactive environment simulator.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Camera features and language-action alignment. Measure on the selected checkpoint and hardware; no normalized cross-method latency here.

Code, dataset, checkpoints, inference.

Raw2Drive

World-model RL

Reported contribution. Aligns raw-sensor and privileged world models to guide a sensor-based driving policy.

Our assessment. Bridges a meaningful input gap. Training access to privileged supervision remains a comparison variable.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Aligned privileged and raw-sensor latent streams. No harmonized inference measurement verified.

Official inference repository; full training release not established.

VLM-SAFE

Semantic safety + world-model RL

Reported contribution. Uses VLM-derived safety labels and a world model for offline, safety-guided policy learning.

Our assessment. Safety-label validity and per-hazard calibration deserve separate testing; semantic guidance alone is not a guarantee.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Latent rollouts with semantic safety estimates. Control-loop latency and hardware require protocol-specific verification.

Author project page with demo/code links.

2026 · ICLR

DriveVLA-W0

World-model supervision for VLA

Reported contribution. Uses dense future-image supervision to improve VLA representation learning and data scaling.

Our assessment. Separate public-data results from additional-data variants. NAVSIM evidence does not establish reactive multi-agent safety.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Discrete visual tokens or continuous visual features. Lightweight action expert targets inference efficiency; compare hardware and input settings.

Code, training instructions, model-weight links.

2026 · February preprint

DriveWorld-VLA

Unified latent world model + VLA

Reported contribution. Unifies scene evolution and planning in a shared latent space without pixel-level rollouts.

Our assessment. Directly relevant to candidate-action reasoning. Audit evaluation reactiveness rather than relying on a closed-loop label.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Shared latent scene and action representations. Latent rollouts avoid pixel synthesis; that does not establish a deployment runtime.

Official repository with environment and evaluation structure.

Paper / author sourceOfficial artifacts
2026 · September preprint

RAF-VLA

Future-representation alignment

Reported contribution. Aligns hidden states with future representations from a pretrained encoder while learning driving actions.

Our assessment. A recent efficiency direction, not equivalent to an explicit action-conditioned simulator. Independent replication remains open.

Representation, runtime, and artifacts

Policy hidden states aligned with future-frame features. Authors report 3.8% training overhead and 1 ms additional inference overhead; not total latency.

Paper verified; code/checkpoint release not verified.

Evaluation landscape

What does the benchmark test?

Strong trajectory or visual prediction metrics are useful evidence. They answer different questions from repeated control in a reactive environment.

Open-loop prediction

Evaluate a trajectory against recorded futures. Useful for accuracy; limited evidence of recovery after the policy changes the scene.

NAVSIM v1

Uses short-horizon, non-reactive scene simulation to compute planning metrics. Other actors do not adapt to the evaluated policy. NAVSIM paper

Pseudo-simulation / NAVSIM v2

Augments real data with synthetic alternative observations and weighting to evaluate recovery behavior. It does not constitute sequential interactive simulation. Pseudo-simulation paper

Reactive CARLA evaluation

Execute repeated controls with evolving traffic. Inspect collisions, signals, progress, timeouts, and intervention cost; simulation-to-real limits remain. Bench2Drive

Checklist for a defensible state-of-the-art claim

Match benchmark version, split, sensor suite, external training data, model selection, rollout horizon, and metric implementation. Distinguish additional-data variants from standard-data results. Report hardware and total latency, not only incremental overhead.

Open research questions · Our assessment

Where reliability remains unresolved.

Model exploitation

A policy may discover actions whose predicted outcomes look safe because learned dynamics are inaccurate. Compare imagined and executed outcomes on policy-induced states.

Calibration under shift

A high aggregate planning score does not establish accurate collision or red-light probabilities on rare or shifted scenarios.

Long-horizon consistency

Small dynamics errors can compound. Separate prediction quality at each horizon from actual planning benefit.

Latency and action alignment

Measure complete observation-to-control latency and whether language, predicted futures, and executed actions agree.

Connection to Study 01

A safety layer around the policy.

SafeAV’s current experiment uses compact privileged-state risk predictors around frozen Behavior Agent and SimLingo policies. It tests the safety trigger formulation; integrating a learned world model is a future direction.
