01 / Research question

Can one safe dimension
hide an unsafe one?

A weighted score can trade one risk against another. We investigate whether independent hazard thresholds improve the safety–progress tradeoff when both methods use the same risk estimates and driving policy.

THE PROBLEM

Risk compensation

Low red-light risk can offset high collision risk in an aggregate trigger. A favorable average may conceal a poorly controlled hazard.

THE HYPOTHESIS

Independent constraints

Trigger intervention when either hazard exceeds its calibrated threshold. Test whether this reduces the worst hazard at comparable progress.

THE EXPERIMENTAL CONTROL

Matched route progress

Sweep thresholds on validation scenarios and compare overlapping progress levels. Measure travel time and braking to reveal excessive conservatism.

System architecture

A frozen policy.
An explicit safety supervisor.

The driving policy proposes a nominal action. A separate supervisor uses calibrated collision and red-light estimates to decide whether to modify throttle and brake.

What stays frozen

The released driving-policy weights. The safety supervisor preserves the base policy’s steering and uses one shared braking mechanism across the scalar and independent variants.

What we learn and evaluate

Compact hazard predictors and calibration mappings are fitted separately. We compare the decision rules under identical scenarios and comparable route progress.

Research approach

What we will investigate.

Both frozen policies receive the same supervisor interface, hazard definitions, intervention mechanism, and paired scenario configurations. No driving-policy training is required by this study.

Scenarios and splits

Collision scenarios

Lead-vehicle braking, cross-traffic conflicts, and supported cut-in events. Late-emerging actors are optional.

Red-light scenarios

Red-signal approaches, signal transitions, and variations in approach speed and intersection geometry.

Hold out entire configurations

Split episodes, routes, configurations, and seeds before training. Use a dedicated calibration set and freeze operating points before testing.

Prediction and control

Compact risk predictors

Use privileged state: ego motion, relative actor motion, traffic signals, stop-line distance, route geometry, and nominal action.

Calibrated probabilities

Predict events within a short horizon selected after pilot analysis. Inspect reliability, Brier score, event counts, and false negatives.

Bounded intervention

Modify throttle and brake while retaining base steering. Both supervisors use the same braking mechanism.

Evaluation metrics

How we will measure success.

Report per-hazard outcomes alongside progress, uncertainty, and intervention cost. Analyze scenario families separately to avoid hiding failures in pooled averages.

Safety

Collision, red-light, any-hazard, and worst-hazard rates. Fix denominators and any normalization before final testing.

Driving utility

Route completion, success, travel time, and progress under a fixed episode time budget.

Intervention cost

Frequency, duration, braking magnitude, jerk, and audited unnecessary interventions.

Statistical comparison

Paired scenarios and seeds, confidence intervals resampled at episode level, and validation-selected operating points.

The primary comparison

Evaluate collision and red-light rates at comparable route completion, with paired episodes and confidence intervals.

Inspect safety–progress curves alongside travel time and intervention cost. A vehicle that simply stops more often should not receive an unqualified safety advantage.

06 / Engineering rigor

Designed to be reproduced.

Each run should connect its observations and decisions to a versioned configuration, seed, route, policy, and scenario definition.

Experiment provenance and artifacts

Record simulator and checkpoint versions, configuration hashes, scenario parameters, split manifests, timestamps, predictor versions, calibration mappings, and supervisor thresholds. Save raw and calibrated risks, nominal and executed actions, trigger reasons, and resulting events.

Assumptions and limits

This is a simulation study using privileged scene state. Probability thresholds are empirical decision rules, not formal safety guarantees. Braking alone cannot resolve every conflict. Calibration can change under distribution shift, and real-world safety remains outside the current claims.

Predictor validity under intervention

Event predictions depend on the future policy. A predictor trained only on unsupervised base-policy rollouts can become miscalibrated once a supervisor changes actions. Evaluate risk on supervisor-induced states and document the continuation policy used to generate labels.

Fair comparisons and failure analysis

Use identical predictors, calibrated outputs, and braking logic for both triggers. Tune scalar weights as carefully as independent thresholds. Compare overlapping progress ranges and travel time. Distinguish risk masking, prediction errors, calibration errors, late intervention, excess braking, and base-policy failures.
